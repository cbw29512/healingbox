Cleric in a Box

A gift from the divine, for the party too stubborn to keep a healer at the table.

Type: a reinforced field chest, roughly the size of a modified bag of holding
Origin: bestowed directly by the campaign's highest deity
Durability: cannot be damaged, destroyed, stolen, opened, or countered by anyone but its divine giver

The box arrived without a note. It only ever needed one line, and the deity never bothered to write it: you will not lose a party to an empty healer's chair again.

What It Does

  • On combat start, the box falls to the ground and springs open on its own — no action required from anyone.
  • When a player drops low, the box hits them with a healing potion as a free action. No roll, no cost, no one's turn spent.
  • If the party needs a specific cleric spell instead of raw healing, the box produces the scroll and reads it aloud itself — see the cost note below the dial.
  • On a long rest, the box's daily allowance refreshes completely, same as a cleric regaining spell slots. A rest of at least 4 hours works if anyone in the party can rest faster; otherwise the usual 8 hours applies.
  • Nothing players or monsters do can affect the box — not the potions inside it, not its healing math, not its ability to keep functioning. Only its divine giver can alter or revoke it.

    Assumptions Baked Into the Math

    • Spellcasting modifier assumed: +3 at levels 1–3, +4 at levels 4–7, +5 at levels 8 and up (typical Wisdom progression with two ASIs invested).
    • 2014 rules: Cure Wounds/Healing Word use 1d8 (or 1d4) per spell level. 2024 rules: dice are doubled per the revised Player's Handbook.
    • 6th-level slots and higher are spent on Heal (70 hp + 10 per slot level above 6th) rather than an upcast Cure Wounds, since it's strictly better.
    • A drawn scroll costs a number of potions equal to that spell's level — a homebrew conversion, not official rules text. Adjust ASSUMPTIONS in script.js if your table wants different numbers.
